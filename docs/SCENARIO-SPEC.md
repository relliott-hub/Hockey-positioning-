# Scenario Spec — for coach review

Every claim this game makes about hockey positioning, in plain language.
Positions are in feet on a regulation 200 × 85 ft sheet.

**What to check:**

1. Would this situation actually happen in a game?
2. Is the **best** read the one you would teach?
3. Is each **acceptable** read genuinely defensible — something you would not correct a player for?
4. Is each **wrong** read a real mistake, and is the reason given the right reason?

Anything you disagree with, mark it and it gets changed. The game grades
strictly against what is written here, so this document *is* the hockey.

### Breakout under pressure

*Breakout · `breakout_corner_pressure`*

Your left defender has retrieved the puck in the corner with a forechecker closing. The team needs to get out of the zone cleanly.

**Puck:** in your defensive zone, toward the near side (17 ft from the end boards, 28 ft near of centre)  
**Carried by:** LD  
**Attacking:** left to right

**Starting positions**

| Player | Where |
|---|---|
| LD *(has the puck)* | in your defensive zone, toward the near side (17 ft from the end boards, 28 ft near of centre) |
| RD | in your defensive zone, toward the far side (19 ft from the end boards, 11 ft far of centre) |
| C | in your defensive zone, toward the near side (27 ft from the end boards, 8 ft near of centre) |
| LW | in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (38 ft from the end boards, 32 ft near of centre) |
| RW | in your defensive zone, toward the far side (55 ft from the end boards, 19 ft far of centre) |

#### Playing LW

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (38 ft from the end boards, 32 ft near of centre)

> On the wall at the hash marks. It's a short, safe pass up the boards and you're already facing up the ice.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (54 ft from the end boards, 34 ft near of centre)
  > Still an outlet on the wall, and it works — but it's a longer pass under pressure, and if your D gets hemmed in you're too far away to help.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (22 ft from the end boards, 30 ft near of centre)
  > You dropped into the corner with your own D. Now one forechecker covers both of you and there's nobody to pass to.
- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (30 ft from the end boards, 2 ft near of centre)
  > Never cut across the front of your own net on a breakout. A turnover there goes straight in.

#### Playing C

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, toward the near side (27 ft from the end boards, 8 ft near of centre)

> Swing low through the middle. You're the closest option, you're moving with speed, and you can take it either way.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (46 ft from the end boards, 2 ft near of centre)
  > You're an option in the middle lane and that can work — but you're further from the puck, so it's a harder pass with a forechecker on your D.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (38 ft from the end boards, 30 ft near of centre)
  > You went to the same wall as your winger. Two players in one lane means one checker covers both.
- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (74 ft from the end boards, 2 ft near of centre)
  > You've left the zone early. Your D has no short option and has to dump it away.

#### Playing RW

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, toward the far side (55 ft from the end boards, 19 ft far of centre)

> Wide on the weak side with speed. You stretch their coverage and you're ready to take a pass going forward.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (50 ft from the end boards, 1 ft far of centre)
  > Middle-lane support does give your team an option — but the middle is crowded here, and that pass gets picked off more often than the wide one.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (26 ft from the end boards, 18 ft near of centre)
  > You collapsed all the way back to the puck. Now everyone is below it and there's nobody to break out to.
- in the neutral zone, toward the far side (79 ft from the end boards, 23 ft far of centre)
  > Too far ahead. The pass can't reach you and you've taken yourself out of the play.

#### Playing RD

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, toward the far side (19 ft from the end boards, 11 ft far of centre)

> On the other side of the net for the D-to-D. If the strong side is jammed, this is the safe reset.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, toward the far side (30 ft from the end boards, 21 ft far of centre)
  > You're available for a pass and that's fine — but a little higher means a longer pass, and you can't get back as quickly if it's lost.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the far side (58 ft from the end boards, 13 ft far of centre)
  > You left up the ice before the puck did. If it's turned over there's only one defender back.
- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (17 ft from the end boards, 14 ft near of centre)
  > Both defenders on the same side of the net. The whole weak side is open now.

### Three on two

*Rush · `rush_three_on_two`*

Your centre is carrying the puck through the neutral zone with numbers. Two defenders are back. Where you go decides whether this becomes a chance.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in the neutral zone, toward the near side (122 ft from the end boards, 18 ft near of centre)

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

## How a choice is graded

A player's placement is judged by **which written read it is nearest to** —
not by any geometric rule. That is deliberate: the previous version scored
positions with distance formulas, and an audit found **35% of the answers it
marked wrong broke none of its own coaching rules**. Every option a player
sees now comes from this document.

| Tier | Score | What the player is told |
|---|---|---|
| Best | 92–100 | The read succeeded, plus the reason it's right |
| Acceptable | 68–78 | "That works" — plus what the stronger read gives you |
| Wrong | 8–38 | The specific mistake, and where they wanted to be |

## How outcomes are decided

Position improves the odds; it does not decide the result.

| Read | Goal | Play stays alive | Lost possession | Goal against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best | 62% | 38% | — | — |
| Acceptable | 24% | 62% | 14% | — |
| Wrong | — | — | 72% | 28% |

A good read creating a goal every single time would teach that being in the
right place guarantees the result, which is not true of the sport.
