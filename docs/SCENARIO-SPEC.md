# Scenario Spec — for coach review

Every claim this game makes about hockey positioning, in plain language.
Positions are in feet on a regulation 200 × 85 ft sheet.

**What to check:**

1. Would this situation actually happen in a game?
2. Is the **best** read the one you would teach?
3. Is each **acceptable** read genuinely defensible — something you would not correct a player for?
4. Is each **wrong** read a real mistake, and is the reason given the right reason?

Anything you disagree with, mark it and it gets changed. The game grades
strictly against what is written here, so this document *is* the hockey.

## The principles being taught

Every read below is tagged with the idea underneath it. The game names the
principle when it gives feedback, and the coach report shows which ones a
player has absorbed and which keep costing them — because the idea is what
transfers to a real game, not the picture.

| Principle | What a kid is told |
|---|---|
| **Support the puck** | Be somewhere your teammate can pass to. |
| **Protect the middle** | The front of your net is the most dangerous ice. |
| **Stay above the puck** | Don't get caught below it. |
| **Keep your lanes apart** | Two of you in one lane is one checker's job. |
| **Cover the weak side** | Someone has to be there when it comes across. |
| **Don't chase the puck** | If everyone goes to the puck, nobody is anywhere. |
| **Do your own job** | Cover your own man — don't take a teammate's. |

## The season plan

The game ships working through these units in order, and only opens the next
one when the reads in the current one are landing. The order is taken from
published coaching guidance rather than invented: Hockey Canada's U13 pathway
states the rule as **"skills before tactics; tactics before structure"**, its
U11 guidance introduces basic defensive-zone positioning first, and special
teams are held back to U13, where players *"should play in all situations,
power play, penalty kill and special teams"*. So the units run outward from
your own net.

| # | Unit | What it teaches | Scenarios | Reads to pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Protect the house** | Learn the most dangerous ice on the sheet — and who guards it. | dzone_corner_battle, dzone_halfwall_coverage | 6 of 10 |
| 2 | **Get out of your end** | Give your defence somewhere safe to pass, and never through the middle. | breakout_corner_pressure, dzone_faceoff | 6 of 8 |
| 3 | **Move it up the ice** | Attack with speed and width instead of all skating to the puck. | nz_regroup, rush_three_on_two | 6 of 8 |
| 4 | **Own the neutral zone** | Move through the middle of the ice, and take it away from them. | nz_faceoff, nz_beat_the_trap, nz_entry | 7 of 12 |
| 5 | **Take it back** | Win the puck with angles and layers, not by chasing it. | forecheck_1_2_2, nz_forecheck_1_2_2, nz_trap_1_3_1 | 7 of 15 |
| 6 | **Keep it in their end** | Hold possession, and have somebody at the net when the chance comes. | ozone_cycle_support, ozone_faceoff | 6 of 8 |
| 7 | **Get back** | When it goes wrong, get to the defensive side of your man. | backcheck_rush, defend_two_on_one, defend_three_on_two | 7 of 15 |
| 8 | **Special teams** | A man up and a man down — the jobs change, and so do their names. | pp_one_three_one, pk_box | 5 of 8 |

Free play ignores the plan and serves anything, which is the right mode for
someone who already knows the game.

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (33 ft from the nearest end boards, 33 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (62 ft from the nearest end boards, 34 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*
  > Stretching high up the wall is a real route and it beats the forecheck when it works — but it's a long pass under pressure, and if your D gets pinned you're too far away to help.

- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (22 ft from the nearest end boards, 3 ft near of centre)  *(Protect the middle)*

## How a choice is graded

A player's placement is judged by **which written read it is nearest to** —
not by any geometric rule. That is deliberate: the previous version scored
positions with distance formulas, and an audit found **35% of the answers it
marked wrong broke none of its own coaching rules**. Every option a player
sees now comes from this document.

| Tier | Score | What the player is told |
|---|---|---|
| Best | 92–100 | The read succeeded, plus the reason it's right |
| Acceptable | 68–78 | "That works" — plus what the stronger read gives you |
| Wrong | 8–38 | The specific mistake, and where they wanted to be |

## How outcomes are decided

Position improves the odds; it does not decide the result.

| Read | Goal | Play stays alive | Lost possession | Goal against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best | 62% | 38% | — | — |
| Acceptable | 24% | 62% | 14% | — |
| Wrong | — | — | 72% | 28% |

A good read creating a goal every single time would teach that being in the
right place guarantees the result, which is not true of the sport.
