# Scenario Spec — for coach review

Every claim this game makes about hockey positioning, in plain language.
Positions are in feet on a regulation 200 × 85 ft sheet.

**What to check:**

1. Would this situation actually happen in a game?
2. Is the **best** read the one you would teach?
3. Is each **acceptable** read genuinely defensible — something you would not correct a player for?
4. Is each **wrong** read a real mistake, and is the reason given the right reason?

Anything you disagree with, mark it and it gets changed. The game grades
strictly against what is written here, so this document *is* the hockey.

| End dot from goal line | 20 ft | 20 ft |
| End dot from centre | 22 ft | 22 ft |
| Crease radius | 6 ft | 6 ft |

One foot means the same distance in every direction, so a spacing judgement
across the ice is measured the same way as one up the ice.

## The principles being taught

Every read below is tagged with the idea underneath it. The game names the
principle when it gives feedback, and the coach report shows which ones a
player has absorbed and which keep costing them — because the idea is what
transfers to a real game, not the picture.

| Principle | What a kid is told |
|---|---|
| **Support the puck** | Be somewhere your teammate can pass to. |
| **Protect the middle** | The front of your net is the most dangerous ice. |
| **Stay above the puck** | Don't get caught below it. |
| **Keep your lanes apart** | Two of you in one lane is one checker's job. |
| **Cover the weak side** | Someone has to be there when it comes across. |
| **Don't chase the puck** | If everyone goes to the puck, nobody is anywhere. |
| **Do your own job** | Cover your own man — don't take a teammate's. |

### Breakout under pressure

*Breakout · `breakout_corner_pressure`*

Your defenceman has retrieved the puck in the corner, below your own goal line, with a forechecker closing. The team needs to get out of the zone.

**Puck:** behind your own net, tight to the near boards (8 ft from the end boards, 31 ft near of centre)  
**Carried by:** LD  
**Attacking:** left to right

**Starting positions**

| Player | Where |
|---|---|
| LD *(has the puck)* | behind your own net, tight to the near boards (8 ft from the end boards, 31 ft near of centre) |
| RD | behind your own net, toward the far side (7 ft from the end boards, 9 ft far of centre) |
| C | in your defensive zone, toward the near side (26 ft from the end boards, 14 ft near of centre) |
| LW | in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (33 ft from the end boards, 33 ft near of centre) |
| RW | in your defensive zone, toward the far side (58 ft from the end boards, 21 ft far of centre) |

#### Playing LW

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (33 ft from the end boards, 33 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*

> Get to the wall at the hash marks. Short, safe pass up the boards, and you're already turned up the ice.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (62 ft from the end boards, 34 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*
  > Stretching high up the wall is a real route and it beats the forecheck when it works — but it's a long pass under pressure, and if your D gets pinned you're too far away to help.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (12 ft from the end boards, 30 ft near of centre)  *(Don't chase the puck)*
  > You went down to the puck. Now two of you are in one corner, one checker covers both, and there's nobody to pass to.
- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (22 ft from the end boards, 3 ft near of centre)  *(Protect the middle)*
  > Never cut across the front of your own net on a breakout. A turnover there goes straight in.

#### Playing C

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, toward the near side (26 ft from the end boards, 14 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*

> Swing low through the middle. Low and slow, supporting your D — you're the closest option and you get it moving forward.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (50 ft from the end boards, 2 ft near of centre)  *(Support the puck)*
  > Waiting high in the middle lane is a genuine option and it's quick up the ice — but it's a long pass with a forechecker on your D, and if it's picked off you're behind it.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, tight to the near boards (34 ft from the end boards, 32 ft near of centre)  *(Keep your lanes apart)*
  > You went to the same wall as your winger. Two players in one lane is one checker's job.
- in the neutral zone, in the middle of the ice (76 ft from the end boards, 1 ft far of centre)  *(Stay above the puck)*
  > You left the zone early. Your D has no short option and has to throw it away.

#### Playing RW

**Best read** — in your defensive zone, toward the far side (58 ft from the end boards, 21 ft far of centre)  *(Cover the weak side)*

> Wide on the weak side, building speed. You stretch their coverage and you take the pass going forward.

**Also acceptable** *(partial credit — works, but not first choice)*

- in your defensive zone, in the middle of the ice (40 ft from the end boards, 5 ft far of centre)  *(Keep your lanes apart)*
  > Coming into the middle to support does give another option — but that's the crowded lane, and a pass through the middle of your own zone is the one that gets picked off.

**Mistakes**

- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (18 ft from the end boards, 20 ft near of centre)  *(Don't chase the puck)*
  > You collapsed all the way back to the puck. Everyone is below it now and there's nobody to break out to.
- in the neutral zone, toward the far side (86 ft from the end boards, 27 ft far of centre)  *(Stay above the puck)*
  > Too far ahead. The pass can't reach you and you've taken yourself out of the play.

#### Playing RD

**Best read** — behind your own net, toward the far side (7 ft from the end boards, 9 ft far of centre)  *(Support the puck)*

> Behind the net on the far side for the D-to-D. The pass goes behind the goal, never across the front of it — that's what makes it safe.

- in your defensive zone, toward the far side (34 ft from the end boards, 23 ft far of centre)  *(Support the puck)*

**Mistakes**

  > You left up the ice before the puck did. If it's turned over there's one defender back.
- in your defensive zone, toward the near side (12 ft from the end boards, 20 ft near of centre)  *(Cover the weak side)*
  > Both defencemen on the same side of the net. The entire weak side is open.

## How a choice is graded

A player's placement is judged by **which written read it is nearest to** —
not by any geometric rule. That is deliberate: the previous version scored
positions with distance formulas, and an audit found **35% of the answers it
marked wrong broke none of its own coaching rules**. Every option a player
sees now comes from this document.

| Tier | Score | What the player is told |
|---|---|---|
| Best | 92–100 | The read succeeded, plus the reason it's right |
| Acceptable | 68–78 | "That works" — plus what the stronger read gives you |
| Wrong | 8–38 | The specific mistake, and where they wanted to be |

## How outcomes are decided

Position improves the odds; it does not decide the result.

| Read | Goal | Play stays alive | Lost possession | Goal against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best | 62% | 38% | — | — |
| Acceptable | 24% | 62% | 14% | — |
| Wrong | — | — | 72% | 28% |

A good read creating a goal every single time would teach that being in the
right place guarantees the result, which is not true of the sport.
